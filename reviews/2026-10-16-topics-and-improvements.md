# The Annotated Shelf: new topics and a project review

16 October 2026. Written for Kevin, whose brief was "new valuable topics and investigation of ways to improve this project", with learning as the yardstick. Part A proposes what to read next. Part B says what the audits found and what to change. The appendix lists every candidate the scouts found, ranked. Nothing in the pipeline, the charter or the Routine was changed; the one binding edit this session made is a sourcing pointer for the Cochrane run in STATE.md.

## How this was produced

Eight scouts each took one lens (trial-methods classics; global-health institutions; frontline implementation; medicine's history and sociology; science essayists; fiction read as peer literature; judgment, noise and forecasting; economics of science) and checked every anchor text for an open copy. They returned 80 candidates. Four judges scored each one for value, learning, novelty, groundability and fit, and wrote the one thing you would come away knowing; a series editor then cut the survivors into series. Separately, six audits (pipeline, craft drift, state and docs, grounding, the quality loop, listener experience) produced 47 findings, and each finding went to an independent second reader instructed to refute it with the repository open. 42 survived, 5 were dismissed, and most of the survivors had their severity cut and their proposal rewritten into something smaller. Where that happened, Part B says so. The numbers in this document were re-measured by the refuters, not copied from the first pass.

## In short

The sixty-six episodes have taught three things well: how organisations behave, how science is funded and judged, and how nutrition got its facts. They have taught almost nothing about the three things you do with your hands. Nothing on the shelf sits inside a statistical analysis plan; nothing measures the reliability of a trained judgment, which is what episode 065 ended by asking; nothing reads the record a frontline worker sends to a distant office. Five scouts working on separate lenses converged on those same thin places without being told to, and the highest-scoring candidates all live there: the Indian monitoring trials in which the same camera, the same NGO and the same district halved teacher absence for years and did nothing for nurses after sixteen months; the finding that 137 oncologists forecasting live trials in their own subspecialty lost to an algorithm that always says fifty percent; Ware's ECMO trial, nine of nine against six of ten, and the published fight over whether it should have been run at all; Lord's paradox and Freedman's warning on what adjustment does to a randomised estimate.

Those candidates share a second property: they are open journal literature, not books. The books that scored high on value and failed grounding (Kaufman's forest rangers, Tendler's Ceará health agents, Wilson's Bureaucracy) are worth an afternoon with a library card, and the report marks them attended-only. Fiction and philosophy scored high on novelty and low on learning; they belong as interludes between dense series, not as a series. Two of them are time-sensitive if you want them: Shaw's preface on statistical illusions before the Fisher episode (068), and Arrowsmith's plague trial before the streptomycin episode (070).

The recommended order after the Doers close at 085 is: the analysis plan (ten fights behind the document you sign before you unblind), then the judge and the formula (Meehl to Noise to the trial forecasters), then the view from headquarters (Lipsky to Honig), then global health as an institution (McKeown to Ebola), then making doctors (Flexner to the duty-hours trials). The first two are the direct sequels to the evidence series and to 065, and they land on the grant-writing months.

On the project itself, the machine half works and the audits spent most of their effort failing to break it: one episode every weekday since 23 September, a Build commit within nineteen seconds of each, a sign-off chain that has never broken, a lint every script since 012 passes. Quality is protected exactly as far as a regex over one script can protect it, and no further. Every confirmed drift is a cross-episode pattern that no single-file check can see: the closing question you banned in one wording on 27 August has re-formed four times and now reads "The question to carry into X" in seven of the last eight episodes; a narrated source list appeared at episode 060 without anyone choosing it and had grown to 227 words in the stress position by 065; the charter's own checklist words ("flagged as constructed") are now spoken aloud; titles have grown from about a hundred characters to three hundred. The cause is structural. The writer is its own teacher, because the continuity rule tells each run to read the last two scripts in full, which is right for continuity and is also how every habit propagates, and nothing pulls it back, because no listen has reached the repository since episode 024. The project's memory has the same shape: STATE.md is 54 KB of which about 4 KB binds, and its rules live in three places that disagree.

The fixes that need no decision from you are small: a dozen lint patterns and three charter sentences; a rule that spoken numbers come from text the session held, with a dated list of which fetch routes work from the cloud; a cold second-reader pass before commit; a concordance of the shelf's running frames so callbacks stop depending on re-reading old scripts; a one-off rewrite of STATE.md and the done skill so snapshots stop stacking. Five things only you can decide, and they are listed at the end of Part B: whether the narrated bibliography stays, a title cap, whether digits are written out, whether you will file a one-tap "listened" note from your phone, and whether a silent-failure watchdog may open an issue despite your no-notifications rule. One further lever is yours alone: the Routine writes on the environment's default model, a tier below the one your day sessions use, and whether to pin it is a cost-against-quality call.

The one time-bound item was handled: the 16 October run writes Cochrane 1972, whose only online copy is a 94-page image scan the pipeline's text tools cannot read. STATE.md now tells the run to read it page by page with the Read tool, with the page map.

#### 1. The analysis plan: ten fights behind the document you sign before you unblind

A statistical analysis plan is a list of arguments its author promises not to make later. Each promise was won in a published fight between named statisticians over questions older than the document, from what makes an experiment a test at all to when an analysis may say "effect". The queued evidence series ends at SAPs as governance (074); this is the layer underneath, reasons rather than rules.

What you would learn: change scores re-import the baseline imbalance they were meant to remove, and ANCOVA with treatment-by-covariate interactions and HC2 errors cannot lose precision to the unadjusted estimate; the standard error already prices in the imbalance you did not see, so a Table 1 p-value tests chance, not the randomisation; a significant pilot of thirty per arm must overstate the true effect by a computable exaggeration ratio, so an R01 powered on it is built to fail.

#### 2. The judge and the formula: what a score contains

Episode 065 ended on whether a one-point difference in a study-section score could be defended to a second reader. That is Meehl's 1954 question, and seventy years of measurement have answered it: a trained judgment can be scored; it loses to a dull rule at prediction; it is reliable only where the job gives fast, honest feedback; it changes kind once collected in a room; and the people best placed to predict a trial cannot.

What you would learn: a regression fitted to a judge's own past calls outpredicts the judge, because it keeps the policy and drops the inconsistency, and with correlated predictors the weights hardly matter; 137 oncologists forecasting live trials in their own subspecialty lost to an algorithm that always says fifty percent, and co-investigators were no better than outsiders; Fogelholm's panels reached kappa 0.23 after discussion, identical to averaging two reviewers' independent scores.

#### 3. The view from headquarters

The only thing that travels from a frontline worker to a distant centre is a record, and every management problem in a dispersed programme is a question about what the record can carry, a question older than any dashboard. The verdict resists a lesson: measurement is a conditional instrument, and the condition is the verifiability of the output.

What you would learn: the same time-stamp machine, same NGO, same district halved teacher absence for years because the NGO paid the teachers, and did nothing for nurses after sixteen months because the district owned the exemptions; BetterBirth raised observed adherence thirty points across 157,689 births and left the death-and-morbidity composite unchanged; across Honig's fourteen thousand projects, field autonomy beat top-down control only where outputs were hard to verify, so counting contacts is right and counting session quality is theatre.

#### 4. Global health as an institution: what the selective settlement bought

Every global-health institution since 1978 is a position in one argument, whether to do the measurable few or to build the system that could do everything, and the argument predates Alma-Ata: it is the question of what made people stop dying when every candidate cause moved at once. The series opens on that fight and follows the selective side's victory to its stress test in Sierra Leone. The measurement it won with is what his working week is made of.

What you would learn: Walsh and Warren priced a package per head and Alma-Ata had no price, so GOBI won on the only metric while the costing assumed the health-worker legs it displaced; the Millennium Villages' threefold mortality claim was an arithmetic error, corrected to slower than the nation; TB mortality fell for a century before Koch, so McKeown's nutrition verdict is an argument by residual that Szreter re-periodised to municipal sanitation from the same tables.

#### 5. Making doctors: the training programme as an institution

Residencies and fellowships have been reformed by three kinds of document, the inspector's report, the ethnography and the randomised trial, and each saw a different programme. A director inherits all three, plus a question older than any of them: what the institution owes the trainee, the patient in front of them and the knowledge that will treat patients not yet born, and who sets the weighting.

What you would learn: Sherbino's 191 students trained in cognitive forcing made no fewer errors, and physicians labelled more biases in an identical workup once told the diagnosis was wrong; FIRST randomised 117 programmes without resident or patient consent, found 9.1 against 9.0 percent, and the noninferiority margin was the whole argument; Larson's academic R0 sits far above replacement, so the pipeline cannot be for placement, and Flexner's 1913 full-time plan, not the 1910 report, built the salaried clinician with protected time.

#### What the pool says

## Part B: Improvements

### The shape of the problem

The machine half of this project works, and that should be said first because the audits spent most of their effort failing to break it. Sixty-six episodes, one per weekday since 23 September with none missing; a Build commit within 19 seconds of each episode commit; a sign-off chain that has never broken; a lint every script since 012 passes. The craft the lint can see is holding: the banned-phrase list is obeyed rather than gamed, mean sentence length has fallen from 34.5 words to 20.3, BLOOM asides are gone, and within the science series the callbacks are argument, not decoration. The log's Verification lines are honest about what was and was not read, which every fix below depends on.

Read together, the audits say quality is protected exactly as far as a regex over one script can protect it, and no further. Every confirmed drift is a cross-episode pattern. The closing question you said grates was banned in one wording on 27 August and has re-formed four times since, lately as "The question to carry into X" in seven of the last eight episodes. A narrated bibliography appeared at 060 without anyone choosing it and had grown to 227 words in the stress position by 065. The charter's own checklist words ("flagged as constructed") are spoken in three of the last five episodes. None of this is visible to a check that reads one file, and the one reader who could hear it stopped at episode 024 five weeks ago. The Routine has noticed in its own way: it has asked whether the episodes are too dense in nine of the last twelve log entries, of a listener who cannot answer.

Two structural causes explain most of it. The loop is open and the writer is its own teacher: the charter tells each run to read the last two scripts in full, which is the right rule for continuity and also the propagation vector for every habit above, and with no listen since September nothing pulls it back. And the project's memory grows without a policy while its rules live in three places that disagree: STATE.md is 54 KB of which about 4 KB binds, the open-questions list stopped at 052, the decisions of record are frozen at 27 August and contradict the Routine prompt on the word target, and the title rule exists only in the prompt. Each run reads five weeks of superseded summaries and then resolves the contradictions by guessing.

Where the second reader downgraded a finding, this report says so. Grounding went from high to medium: half the last thirty episodes did not read their named anchor, but 13 of 14 spot-checked nutrition figures match the primaries, and the one wrong summary-derived number (059's "seven percent") was hedged on air and corrected in 063. Summariser-derived numbers went to low for the same reason; the feed-size worry is unquantified rather than real; the density finding was refuted as a charter rule and survives only as a listening request. Cochrane is high only because it is time-bound: the series closer fires at 06:07 UTC today against a PDF the pipeline cannot read.
